Compiling frontier perception into cheap readers of live EHR screens

A design for continuously observing live EHR sessions, with clinicians’ knowledge and to their benefit. The capture stream is segmented into recurring screen states, and each state is classified by activity and by whether it is worth extracting from. Frontier-model reads of the worthwhile states accumulate, and compile agents distill them, together with every other available signal, into deterministic parse-based readers that run at negligible cost. Each reader is maintained against a test corpus that grows over time; guards on every frame and a small audit sample feed an exception queue that agents work first and humans finish.

§ 01

Strategy

Six positions that the rest of the design depends on. Each could have gone another way. §03 gives the runtime mechanisms, §04 supplies tactics for the hard cases, and §07 tests them.

S1
Build for EHRs specifically. The target is certified EHR products rather than desktop software in general. That allows a closed taxonomy of clinical activities, recognizable vendor families, known identifier formats (MRN, DOB, CSN), and existing patient-context semantics (CCOW). These priors feed classification, state identity, grammars, and validation throughout.
S2
Classify before extracting. Most frames in a live feed should not be extracted from: logins, menus, transitions, half-typed notes, multi-patient grids. A classification layer assigns each state an activity and decides whether it justifies a compiled reader. States that fail the gate still contribute to the activity timeline, which is a useful output on its own.
S3
Agents distill rote AI reads into deterministic readers, and maintain them like software. This is the central tenet. Frontier reads accumulate as a byproduct of operating (classifies, escalations, audits, pull reads), and compile agents distill them, together with every other available signal (AX and DOM text, OCR streams, database truth on testbeds), into declarative parse-based readers. A per-state test corpus, maintained over time, is the fitness function. An exception queue routes what automation cannot resolve to more AI and then to human review, and every resolution becomes a permanent test case. The runtime that executes the readers stays non-agentic (§02, layer 4).
S4
Budget verification, and hold patient identity to a stricter bar. Deterministic guards run on every frame at no cost. A small adaptive frontier-audit budget covers the failures guards can’t detect. Attributing data to the wrong patient is treated as a safety incident with a target rate of zero, and gets machinery of its own (§03.4, §04 C4).
S5
Design for clinician trust. Continuous observation of EHR work requires clinicians’ ongoing consent, and the design assumes they will inspect it: pixels are processed at the edge and discarded, every extracted value traces to a frame and reader version, and the people being observed get direct value back (ambient context for tools they already use, their own activity timeline). Access granted on these terms is also the part of the system hardest for anyone else to reproduce.
S6
Track the provenance of every design belief. Findings carry a badge (measured, prior, bet), and priors get re-tested whenever the data distribution changes. §00 applies this to the corpus findings; §07 assigns each prior an experiment.
§ 02

Architecture

Layer 1 · Capture

Capture is window-scoped and event-triggered, limited to surfaces established as EHR products (scope: §03.5), on input events (click, end of a keypress burst, scroll settle, focus change) plus a slow heartbeat for passively updating screens. The accessibility tree (UIA, AX, AT-SPI) and DOM (extension or CDP) are recorded synchronously wherever they exist. They provide text, geometry, and roles at no cost, and they supply the alignment signal used to grade the other substrates. Window enumeration and z-order give every frame a visibility mask. VDI and Citrix sessions are detected at capture time from the window class and process; they reduce the substrate ladder to pixels and raise the audit rate (§04, C3). Frames sit in a ring buffer, extractions persist, and pixels are discarded by default.

Layer 2 · Segmentation and state identity

A screen state is what stays constant while patient data varies. Identity is hierarchical: (vendor → module → screen-family → layout-variant), because personalization, roles, zoom, and window width produce many variants of one screen family. Fingerprints come from structure rather than content: the set and arrangement of static tokens that recur across frames. Labels stay put while values change. Settle detection runs per region rather than per frame, so a live-updating widget doesn’t prevent the banner from reaching stability. Frames far from every known signature are treated as novel states and routed to the teacher.

Cross-frame variance within a state does limited but useful work: it separates the static label lexicon from the dynamic zones without any model calls. On elastic layouts it cannot place value boundaries; those come from parsing (layer 4).

Layer 3 · Classification and the extraction gate

Every settled state is classified against a closed EHR activity taxonomy (roughly 30 activities: login, patient-search, schedule, inbox, chart-review:{summary, results, notes, meds…}, order-entry, med-rec, documentation, e-prescribing, billing, admin, …) along with patientScope (single/multiple/none) and identityVisibility. This generalizes the v3 schema’s isEhrScreen / patientScope / singlePatientConfidence trio, which the corpus shows the teacher handles well measured · corpus. The teacher assigns the label once per state; signature matching handles every later visit.

The gate: a state gets a compiled reader when identity is reliably visible, the state recurs often enough to amortize compilation, and the content is worth having. States that fail the gate still feed the activity timeline (which module, what activity, for how long), which is a product surface on its own and the temporal spine for §05’s consistency checks. The unknown-activity bucket is monitored; growth there means the taxonomy has a gap.

Layer 4 · The reader factory

This layer is the center of the design. Every frontier call the system makes for any reason (a novel-state classify, a deopt escalation, an audit re-read, a context-pull read on an uncompiled state) produces a labeled frame, and every labeled frame lands in its state’s corpus. Rote reads accumulate into training and test data as a byproduct of operating. When a state crosses the extraction gate, compile agents distill that corpus into a reader spec, requesting targeted extra reads where the corpus lacks diversity (long names, wrapped banners, collapsed headers) and running the rich config (boxes, thinking, additionalFields) for those. The output is one flat, declarative record per field prior — re-test live (E5):

{ "field":    "patient.dateOfBirth",
  "anchor":   { "text": "DOB", "role": "label" },          // from the state's static lexicon
  "value":    { "extent": "afterAnchor → nextLabel", "line": "same" },
  "grammar":  "date:MM/DD/YYYY",                            // places the boundary when geometry can't
  "posPrior": { "x": [0.32, 0.41], "confidence": "distribution over observed frames" },
  "substrate":["ax", "ocr"],
  "state":    "S-07", "variantOf": "chart-summary", "specVersion": 3 }

The reader is a parser with priors. It locates anchors from the lexicon, takes the value as the span from its label to the next static token on the same visual line, lets the content grammar (dates, MRN patterns, name casing) place the exact boundary, and uses the position distribution to flag outliers. A long value that pushes the rest of the line to the right still parses; the prior records the shift. Geometry resolves ambiguity when a label appears twice, as on a two-patient compare screen, and has no other authority.

Readers are declarative specs executed by a single engine, which keeps them diffable, versionable, and reviewable. Model-generated code is permitted only as a quarantined escape hatch for states the spec language can’t express. Before promotion, every reader must pass an auto-generated test suite: held-out frames from the same state, graded against the teacher’s extractions of them.

Each state accumulates three durable artifacts. Mechanical derivation (union the boxes, pick anchors from the lexicon, attach grammars) covers well-behaved states; compile agents handle the rest, drawing on everything the state has: rote frontier runs, AX and DOM dumps, OCR token streams, database truth on testbeds, and past adjudications. The artifacts:

1 · The reader spec. The deterministic extractor itself, versioned as described above.

2 · A test corpus that grows over time. Frames paired with expected values, drawn from every truth source available. It starts as the teacher’s own labels and gains a case from every exception afterward. A recompile after drift must pass the accumulated corpus or explicitly retire cases under review: regression protection in the ordinary software sense, applied to perception.

3 · An exception queue. Guard failures, audit disagreements, and test failures land here. Agents triage first: reproduce the failure, decide whether it is a reader bug, layout drift, or a wrong expected value, and propose a spec revision or a new test case. What agents cannot resolve goes to human review. Every resolution returns to the corpus as a permanent case, so the same failure never needs the queue twice.

Compile agents iterate propose, test, diagnose, revise against the corpus; the test suite is the fitness function, not only a final gate. Drift recompiles (C7) run the same loop seeded with the old spec and the layout diff, and the codegen escape hatch lives inside it. All of this is safe because promotion is indifferent to authorship: a reader ships only by passing the corpus, however it was written, and verification is deterministic. Compile time also has none of the runtime’s constraints; it is offline, per state, and a bad outcome is a wasted compile rather than a wrong extraction. The runtime stays non-agentic throughout: agents build and maintain the decoders, and a single trusted engine runs them. The repo already runs its hunt-and-abstract sweep as a multi-agent workflow with a worker pool and a resumable per-item ledger; the compile farm is the same shape, with one state per agent and the exception queue as its work feed.

Deterministic validators run at every tier: grammar conformance, date plausibility, MRN patterns, and checksums where they exist. They provide per-extraction confidence and never themselves need auditing.

Layer 6 · The loop

§ 03

Algorithms: cheap classification and patient-context tracking

§02 names the layers. This section specifies the mechanisms that keep the per-frame path in the millisecond range and make the active-patient estimate explicit. The constants given are starting points bet; E1/E2 tune them.

3.1 · The per-frame fast path

Every frame gets a fixed budget of a few milliseconds, spent on hashes rather than models. The frame is divided into a 16×12 tile grid with a 64-bit perceptual hash (dHash) per tile: about 1.5 KB per frame, and comparison against the previous frame is 192 XORs. Three outcomes:

No tiles changed. Drop the frame.
Changes confined to the current state’s dynamic zones. A content event. When the affected regions settle, run the readers for the fields in those zones and pass the results to the context filter (3.4).
Changes touching static zones. A transition candidate. Wait for settle, then run state identification (3.2).

Settle is per region: two consecutive frames under a Hamming threshold, with zones the state has marked volatile (clocks, spinners) exempted so they can’t hold the rest of the screen hostage. The banner tripwire from C4 is this same mechanism pointed at one region: the tiles under the compiled banner zone. Any change there flags a possible patient switch immediately, before any OCR runs.

Where the zone map comes from. A state’s static/dynamic map is learned, not given. On a state’s first visits there is no map, so every change is a transition candidate and goes through identification (3.2). Each time identification returns the same state, the tiles that changed are recorded as dynamic, since they changed while identity held. Tiles that also churn within a single visit (spinners, clocks, a region being typed into) are marked volatile and excluded from settle logic. Tiles that stay constant across many visits become static. The fast path is therefore an optimization that converges over visits, with identification as the always-correct fallback; a new state pays the 30–200 ms path more often until its map fills in.

Two qualifications. Within one visit the patient does not change, so patient fields look static; only comparison across visits exposes them as value-bearing, which is why the map keys on cross-visit change. And the map says where content changes, never what it means: naming a dynamic zone (this one is the identity banner, that one is a census count) is the teacher’s job at compile time, or the AX tree’s where present. Unnamed dynamic zones are tracked for change classification but never read. A static tile that starts changing while the signature still matches the same state is the drift signal from C7 and routes to recompile.

3.2 · State identification at transitions

Identification runs a few times per minute, not per frame. Token gathering uses the AX/DOM dump when available (milliseconds, exact) and platform OCR on the settled frame otherwise. Tokens are normalized (case-folded, digits mapped to #), positions quantized to a 32×24 grid, and the signature is a MinHash (128 permutations) over the set of (token, cell) pairs. Known states live in an LSH index; lookup stays sub-millisecond into the thousands of states.

Decision rule: estimated Jaccard at or above roughly 0.6 against the best candidate, with a clear margin over the runner-up, means a known state. High token overlap with poor positional agreement means the same screen family in a new layout variant; that path re-estimates geometry priors against the family vocabulary, which is much cheaper than a full teacher compile. Anything else is a novel state and goes to the teacher queue.

For a known state, activity, patientScope, identityVisibility, and the reader are fields on the state record. Per-frame activity classification therefore costs nothing at runtime; it was paid once when the teacher labeled the state. A novel state gets a lean classify call (~$0.003) on first sight to assign those labels; full compilation waits until its recurrence counter crosses the gate. Until a state is classified, it appears in the activity timeline as unknown and nothing is extracted from it.

This keeps rarely-visited screens from becoming blind spots in context tracking without paying to compile them.

3.4 · The active-patient estimate

The single most valuable output is the current patient, which typically holds for minutes at a time and degrades with uncertainty rather than ending crisply. It is modeled as an explicit belief per EHR window: {patientRef, confidence, basis, lastConfirmed}. The belief is cheap to maintain, hard to raise, and easy to lose.

Evidence and its effect on confidence:

EventEffect on the belief
AX-sourced banner read, two concordant identifiersset patient, conf ≈ 0.99
OCR banner read, two concordant identifiers, grammars passset patient, conf ≈ 0.97
read with one identifier, or marginal validator scorescap at 0.8; request a second-identifier read

banner tripwire fires (hash change in banner zone)demote to switch-suspected; force fresh read at next settle; assert nothing meanwhile

Between evidence events, maintenance depends on what is visible. While the banner zone is on screen and its hash is stable, confidence holds without decay; the tripwire carries the guarantee. When identity is not on screen (a module without a banner, occlusion, the EHR unfocused), confidence decays exponentially, with half-lives set per situation bet: banner hidden but EHR focused, about 3 minutes; EHR in the background, about 10; lock or disconnect, immediate reset. The asymmetry is deliberate and matches C4: raising context requires an identifier quorum, while lowering it requires only doubt. A wrong “patient A” is a safety event; a wrong “unknown” costs one extra read.

The tripwire is asymmetric in what it establishes. A stable banner hash is a cheap certificate of continuity: the same pixels are on screen, so the same name and MRN are on screen, and confidence holds with no model in the loop. A changed hash establishes nothing about direction; it may be a switch, a redraw, a highlight, or a theme change. It only moves the belief to switch-suspected, and a fresh read settles the question. The system never concludes a switch from pixels alone; it detects loss of certainty from pixels and restores certainty with a read. This is the same pattern the readers use: verify once with an expensive read, then monitor cheaply for invalidation. For patient-bound states without a compiled reader, the identity zone is wherever the interpreter read found the identifiers, and that region is hashed from then on.

on_frame(frame):
  delta = tile_diff(frame, prev)                      # 3.1, ~1 ms
  if banner_zone.intersects(delta): context.suspect_switch()
  if delta.within(state.dynamic_zones):
    for zone in settled(delta): context.integrate(run_readers(zone))
  elif settled(delta):
    state = identify(frame)                           # 3.2, 30–200 ms
    if state.patient_bound: context.require_fresh_read()

context.integrate(read):
  if read.concordant_identifiers >= 2: set(read.patient, conf = read.strength)
  elif read.matches(current.patient):  refresh(cap = 0.97)
  else:                                demote()       # disagreement: no context until resolved

every_second():
  if banner_visible and banner_hash_stable: return    # tripwire holds confidence
  conf *= decay(situation)                            # half-life per situation
  if conf < consumer.threshold: report_unknown()

Consumers get {patient, confidence, ageSeconds, basis} and set their own thresholds: a scribe might require 0.95 and under 30 seconds of age, while retrospective analytics accepts far less. Below threshold the answer is unknown, never a guess. The filter as written is a degenerate Bayes filter over a handful of hypotheses (the current patient, a specific other patient when a row was clicked, none); if E7’s calibration measurements show the heuristic version misbehaving, the full probabilistic treatment is a contained upgrade.

3.5 · Scope: deciding whether the EHR is on screen at all

The scope decision exists for correctness: an email, a PDF, or a portal message must not be analyzed as an EHR screen. This failure is easy to reach with naive gates, because clinical email is full of EHR-shaped content. A referral message can carry a patient name, a DOB, and an MRN, and a lexical test would happily read it as a banner. Scope is therefore decided by what application is on screen, with word-level evidence in a supporting role at most.

The core pathway is visual and works anywhere. The deployment that matters most sees one opaque Citrix window with no process names, no URLs, and no DOM, so scope cannot depend on any of those. Every settled surface goes through the same signature machinery as states (3.2), and the index holds negative entries alongside positive ones: Outlook is examined once, classified as not an EHR surface, and afterwards recognized by signature at the fast path and skipped, exactly like a known state. Structural evidence dominates this classification, since vendor chrome and layout signatures generalize across screens of the same product, while lexical content stays supporting evidence, for the reason above. A surface that stays unrecognized contributes foreground time to the activity timeline and nothing else: no readers, no context updates.

Metadata is an accelerator, never a dependency. Where process names, window classes, tab URLs, or window titles exist (native installs, browser EHRs, published-app Citrix titles), they shortcut the same decision at zero cost and help seed the catalog from the CHPL product list. A deployment can run pixels-only end to end and reach the same answers, later and at slightly higher cost. When any pathway suggests an unlisted EHR surface (a new module, an unexpected domain), it is proposed for confirmation rather than silently included.

Ambiguity resolves through the same teacher gate as everything else. A surface that metadata and signatures cannot place gets the lean classify call, and isEhrScreen is part of it. The corpus exercised that classifier only on near-misses among product screenshots (826 of 828 positive), so its discrimination against email and documents is untested prior — re-test live and is measured in E2. Until a surface classifies as an EHR screen, nothing is extracted from it.

Defense in depth for the residual case. Suppose an email slips through anyway. It still cannot set patient context: context assertions come only from states classified as patient-bound EHR screens (3.4), readers exist only for states that passed the taxonomy gate, and an identifier quorum from an unclassified surface is never accepted. A mis-scoped surface can waste a classify call; it cannot inject a patient.

§ 04

Real-world challenges

Specific conditions in live EHR use that break simple designs. Each card states the condition, the strategic posture, and the tactics. The architecture in §02 and the mechanisms in §03 already embed these; the experiments in §07 test them.

C1 · Elastic banners

“LI, BO” and “ANDERSON-MARTINEZ, GUADALUPE” put the MRN in different places. Long banners wrap to two lines. Headers collapse and expand. Value boxes change size and position from frame to frame, so a reader cannot memorize coordinates.

Parse the banner as a token stream. Use geometry for priors and disambiguation only.

tactics
  • Value extent runs from its label to the next static-lexicon token on the same visual line.
  • Content grammars (date, MRN, name casing) place exact boundaries.
  • Position distributions over observed frames; an outlier raises a flag instead of failing the read.
  • Compile from k frames selected for value-length diversity, wrapped and unwrapped.
C2 · One screen, many layouts

Epic users personalize chart tabs and columns. Different roles see different panes. Workstations run 100/125/150% zoom at assorted window widths. A single screen family produces many layout variants.

Give states hierarchical identity, share knowledge at the family level, and compile a variant only when its frequency justifies it.

tactics
  • Identity = (vendor, module, screen-family, layout-variant); the family is recognized from label-lexicon overlap.
  • Field vocabulary attaches to the family; geometry priors attach to the variant.
  • Zoom is estimated from known anchor-pair distances and normalized out.
  • Low-frequency variants stay on the interpreter path instead of being compiled.
C3 · Citrix and VDI delivery

Large health systems deliver Epic and Oracle Health through Citrix. The capture host sees one opaque window, with no accessibility tree or DOM, and H.264 compression smears 8-point MRN digits.

Treat pixels-only as the baseline the system must work on. Side channels are an upgrade where present. Weaker substrates get larger audit budgets.

tactics
  • Detect VDI sessions at capture (window class, process) and tag every downstream record.
  • Aggregate OCR across settled frames; stacking the same static region cancels much of the compression noise.
  • Apply per-substrate audit multipliers; lean harder on grammar validators.
  • Where a partnership allows, run the agent inside the VDI image and recover the full ladder.
C4 · Wrong-patient attribution

A clinician flips between two charts, and extraction lag attributes patient A’s screen to patient B. In this domain that is a safety incident.

Patient identity is a protected field class with its own machinery. When in doubt, attribute nothing.

tactics
  • A pixel diff over the banner region runs on every frame as a context-change tripwire, cheaper than any extraction.
  • Context is asserted only on two concordant identifiers (name plus MRN or DOB); frames near a switch are quarantined.
  • An explicit context state machine allows one active patient, with transitions only through search and select states. This matches the CCOW model, inferred from the screen.
  • Misattribution counts as a safety event in every eval. The target is zero.
C5 · Multi-patient surfaces

Schedule grids with thirty patients, results inboxes, and side-by-side compare views are full of identities that must not become the session’s patient context.

Multi-patient states are read row-by-row or logged as activity only. They never set patient context.

tactics
  • patientScope classification per state (already demonstrated in v3) selects the behavior.
  • Row readers: vertical periodicity locates the repeating row template, and each row carries its own identity.
  • Compare views combine C1’s duplicate-label handling with C4’s quarantine rules.
C6 · Dirty frames and occlusion

Skeleton loaders, spinners, half-typed notes, dropdowns covering the banner, toasts, an Outlook window across half the chart, sticky-note widgets.

Extract only from settled, unoccluded regions. Treat in-progress edits as activity signal rather than content.

tactics
  • Per-region settle detection; a typing burst defers the region it touches until blur or settle.
  • Overlay detection: a new rectangle on top of a known state is classified separately, and the area beneath it is masked.
  • Each field checks the window z-order mask for visibility before it is read.
  • Partial-signature matching lets an occluded state still classify.
C7 · Drift and upgrades

Vendors ship quarterly, organizations re-theme, and a user can rearrange their chart tabs overnight. Every reader assumes yesterday’s layout.

Assume drift will happen. Detect it deterministically and recover by recompiling rather than by widening tolerances.

tactics
  • The signature-drift guard runs per frame, so deoptimization happens before a wrong read rather than at the next audit.
  • Canary fields are audited on a schedule regardless of agreement history.
  • Specs are versioned with validity windows; recompile diffs are reviewed (a moved field is routine, a vanished field is an alarm).
  • Another site on the same (vendor, version) warm-starts the recompile through the registry (§08).
C8 · Scarce, PHI-bound ground truth

Real frames can’t go to a labeling service. AX truth disappears under VDI. §00 shows the frontier model itself shares some errors with OCR.

Use the sources of truth that cost nothing, build synthetic ones where that is safe, and reserve human attention for adjudicating disagreements.

tactics
  • AX/DOM text is authoritative wherever present, and grades both OCR and the frontier model.
  • Synthetic testbeds: open-source EHRs (OpenEMR, Bahmni) loaded with Synthea patients, driven by scripted sessions, where the database supplies the labels.
  • An on-site adjudication queue handles persistent disagreements and doubles as the labeled eval set.
  • Deterministic validators backstop everything; second-model audits are used sparingly.
§ 05

Verification

Two channels with different budgets and different failure classes: deterministic guards that run on every frame, and a metered audit sample. Patient identity additionally gets the stricter regime described in C4.

Guards
deterministic · always-on · ~free
  • anchor fails to resolve in the state’s lexicon
  • grammar or validator fails (date, MRN pattern, checksum)
  • OCR confidence below floor; position far outside the prior
  • tier 0 (AX) and tier 1 (OCR) disagree
  • layout signature drifts past threshold
  • state is novel, or the occlusion mask covers the field
  • banner tripwire: patient context changed without a search/select transition

Any trigger escalates the frame to the frontier model. Repeated triggers on one (state, field) cause a recompile and a spec version bump. Frontier spend follows actual failures.

Sampled audit
statistical · adaptive · metered
  • the frontier model re-reads x% of cleanly extracted frames (lean config) and the results are compared field by field
  • x is tracked per (state, field, substrate): a fresh reader starts near 25%, decays toward 0.5–1% as agreement holds, and rises again on any disagreement
  • pixel-only substrates (VDI) carry a standing multiplier
  • budget concentrates where cheap signals already look off: substrate disagreement, thin validator margins

Audits catch the case guards can’t: a reader that confidently parses the wrong span, such as an anchor that matched the other “DOB” on a compare screen.

Both channels drain into the per-state exception queue (§02, layer 4): agents triage each flagged frame, resolutions become permanent test cases in the state’s corpus, and the residue goes to human review. The adjudication queue in the callout below is the human end of that same pipeline.

What auditing cannot catch. Auditing keeps the cheap path consistent with the frontier model. It cannot catch an error both make, and §00 shows those exist (identical digit misreads in both configs). The defenses, in increasing cost: grammar and checksum validators, which reject impossible values regardless of who produced them; AX-tree text where present, which is the application stating the string rather than a perception of it; evaluation on synthetic testbeds where the database supplies the labels (C8); occasional second-model audits; and a small human adjudication queue, which doubles as the labeled eval set. Temporal consistency adds one more check: a date of birth that changes mid-session means a reader broke or a context switch was missed, and either way the frame escalates.

The observability surface extends the current viewer’s coverage report: agreement rates per (state, field, substrate), deopt counts, audit spend, reader version history, drift alarms, and the wrong-patient count, each clickable through to the underlying frames. This is also the concrete form of S5: clinicians and their organizations can see what was read, from which screen, and with what confidence.

A clinician’s day produces a few thousand settled frames, concentrated in a few dozen state-variants, and an organization concentrates further. A variant seen 10,000 times amortizes its compile cost to noise. The policy is to track variant frequency, compile lazily, and leave the long tail on the interpreter path, since the lean frontier config is cheap enough for one-off reads. The open question that live feeds add is variant count (C2): personalization, zoom, and role could multiply compile targets. Hierarchical identity limits the damage, since vocabulary is shared at the family level and only geometry priors are per-variant, and compilation is frequency-gated. E2 measures the actual variant distribution.

Steady-state spend reduces to the audit budget, which is the main tunable: it rises where agreement hasn’t been established and decays where it holds.

Cost and trust are the same design. The properties that make extraction cheap also make the system acceptable to host. In steady state, pixels are processed and discarded at the edge; only the k compile frames and the audit samples reach a frontier model, cropped to the regions needed. Together with per-value traceability, this is what clinicians and security teams reasonably ask to see before granting, and renewing, permission to observe clinical work (S5).
§ 07

Experiment program

Ordered so each result gates the next. Every prior — re-test live badge from §00 has an experiment here. E1 builds the shared infrastructure: a live testbed where the database supplies ground truth.

E1
Live testbed: open-source EHR with synthetic patients
hypothesisA real EHR (OpenEMR or Bahmni) loaded with Synthea patients and driven through scripted clinical sessions yields ground-truth-labeled live capture, since every on-screen value is known from the database.
methodBrowser and desktop capture rig (frames, AX/DOM side channels, input events); Playwright-scripted sessions covering login, schedule, charts, orders, and notes, across patients with deliberately short and long names.
metricCoverage: the share of on-screen truth values recoverable from the capture stream by any means, frontier included.
decisionInfrastructure for everything after it. Also re-measures per-call capture stochasticity (the ~30% prior) on live frames.
E2
Segmentation and state identity on live streams
hypothesisRecurring-token signatures with per-region settle detection carve a session into clean states, and the variant distribution across zoom, window size, and personalization is shallow enough to compile against.
methodRun E1 streams, plus re-runs at 100/125/150% zoom, three window widths, and two user configurations, through segmentation. Interleave out-of-scope activity (email, browsing, both windowed and inside the simulated VDI desktop) to exercise the scope gate (§03.5). Hand-grade state boundaries and cluster purity. Count distinct variants per screen family.
metricBoundary precision and recall; cluster purity; variants-per-family histogram; scope misclassification, with particular attention to clinical email carrying patient-shaped tokens. The target is zero non-EHR surfaces reaching extraction or context updates.
decisionPurity above 90% with shallow variant tails supports hierarchical identity as designed. Deep tails move family-level normalization (C2) ahead of any compilation work.
E3
Activity taxonomy and the extraction gate
hypothesisA closed taxonomy of roughly 30 EHR activities covers real sessions, and the teacher assigns (activity, patientScope, identityVisibility) reliably enough to gate extraction.
methodThe teacher labels every E1/E2 state; two human graders adjudicate. Measure the unknown-activity residual. Simulate the gate against ground truth of whether identity was actually extractable in each state.
metricTaxonomy coverage; label agreement; gate precision and recall. The two error directions cost differently (a false pass wastes compile spend, a false fail loses data) and the threshold is tuned accordingly.
decisionFixes taxonomy v1 and the gate threshold. A large unknown residual sends the taxonomy back for revision before scale-out.
E4
Reader models under elastic layouts
hypothesisParse-based readers (lexicon, extent rules, grammars, position priors) outperform fixed-offset readers on elastic layouts and approach teacher accuracy on settled frames.
methodCompile both reader types for each E2 state, each in two variants: mechanical derivation only, and with the agentic refine loop on test failures (§02, layer 4). Evaluate on held-out frames spanning name lengths, wraps, zooms, and widths. Ablate each component: grammar off, priors off, extent only.
metricThe two numbers the design rests on: the share of fields that compile at all, and per-field reader accuracy against E1 database truth rather than against the teacher.
decisionAt or above 80% compiled and 95% accuracy on compiled fields, the compile approach stands for live feeds. Below that, the failure taxonomy (anchor miss, extent error, grammar gap, unstable layout) directs the next iteration.
E5
Schema shape, re-tested
hypothesisThe corpus finding that flat schemas extract better (§00) may hold, weaken, or reverse on live-feed teacher passes with current models.
methodTeacher passes over E1 states with flat, moderately nested, and per-state-extended schemas. Same frames, same budget. Grade against database truth.
metricPer-field capture rate and cost, by schema shape and state density.
decisionSets the spec and teacher-schema policy on live evidence. Either outcome retires the badge; the belief becomes measured.
E6
VDI degradation
hypothesisCompression costs accuracy in measurable and partly recoverable ways: multi-frame OCR aggregation restores much of it, and audit multipliers cover the remainder.
methodReplay E1 sessions through a Citrix-like transport (H.264 at several bitrates). Strip the side channels. Run the pixel-only ladder with and without frame stacking.
metricPer-field accuracy versus bitrate; recovery attributable to aggregation; the residual error class, digits in particular.
decisionSets per-substrate audit multipliers and a minimum viable bitrate. If digits stay unreliable, MRN-bearing fields under VDI get mandatory audit or abstention.
E7
Wrong-patient drill
hypothesisThe context state machine, banner tripwire, and identifier quorum produce zero misattributions under adversarial navigation.
methodScripted rapid chart-flipping, compare views, mid-load switches, and look-alike names (Synthea twins) on the E1 testbed. Every extraction is attributed and checked against the driving script.
metricMisattribution count, with a target of zero; the abstention rate paid to get there; and calibration of the context filter (observed accuracy at each reported confidence level, §03.4).
decisionGates any deployment where extracted context feeds other tools. On failure, the C4 machinery is reworked before anything else scales.
E8
Drift and recompile
hypothesisAn upgrade or re-theme of the testbed EHR is caught by the signature guard with no silently wrong reads during the gap, and recompilation restores service at low cost.
methodUpgrade OpenEMR, swap themes, and rearrange a user’s layout mid-stream. Measure detection latency, deopt behavior, recompile cost, and spec-diff quality.
metricCount of silently wrong reads during the transition, with a target of zero. Deopting to the frontier during the gap is acceptable; wrong output is not.
decisionGates unattended operation across vendor release cycles.
E9
Maintenance soak
hypothesisWith the factory loop running (compile agents, growing test corpora, exception queue), exception volume decays over weeks of operation and repeat failures approach zero.
methodRun the full system on the E1 testbed for several simulated weeks with scheduled churn: content variety, the E8 drift injections, personalization changes. Agents triage the queue; humans handle the residue; every resolution becomes a corpus case.
metricExceptions per 1,000 frames over time; agent-resolution rate versus human residue; repeat-failure rate (the same root cause reaching the queue twice); corpus growth against coverage.
decisionThis tests the central tenet (S3). If exception volume does not decay, the factory is not learning and the maintenance model needs rework before any real deployment.
§ 08

Longer-term vision

now → near
Testbed phase

E1 through E5 on synthetic-patient testbeds: the compile approach, the reader model, and the classification layer get tested against database-grade ground truth. The static corpus remains useful as a cross-vendor prior; 238 vendors’ worth of banner conventions seed the taxonomy and the grammars.

near
First live deployment, with clinicians who opt in

A small deployment where participating clinicians get something back immediately: their own activity timeline, and ambient patient context feeding a tool they already use (a scribe, an assistant, a summary panel). The verification dashboard is open to them. S5 either holds up here or the design needs rework before anything larger.

mid
A shared reader registry

Reader specs contain layout knowledge and no patient data, so they can be shared: a registry keyed by (vendor, product, version, screen-family) that warm-starts any new site running software the registry has seen, open to community audit the way the current viewer exposes every abstraction. Test corpora built on synthetic testbeds travel with the specs; corpora built from real sites stay local. CHPL is a natural index, since it already enumerates the certified products.

mid → far
Beyond the banner

Clinical body content (medication lists, results tables, scrolling grids) as new reader types: row models, table grammars, scroll stitching, on the same classify/compile/guard/audit loop. The activity timeline matures into workflow analytics measured at the workstation rather than inferred from vendor logs.

far
Ambient clinical context

The context-synchronization layer CCOW specified, produced from the screen instead of from vendor integration: every tool at the workstation knows the current patient, encounter, and activity, emitted as FHIR (Patient, Encounter, Provenance), with each value traceable to a frame, a reader version, a substrate, and its audit history. It rests on the two things this design accumulates: compiled knowledge of certified EHR surfaces, and the continued consent of the people whose screens it reads.
